## ABSTRACT

This paper proposes the Network State Environmental, Social, and Governance Framework (NS-ESG), the first composite sustainability reporting standard purpose-built for Network States — geographically distributed, voluntary, digitally-native communities that operate across multiple sovereign jurisdictions without a contiguous territory or a single recognised legal form. NS-ESG completes a four-paper research programme that began with the Network State Classification Framework (NSCF v2.1, 10.5281/zenodo.19561980), continued with the Network State Credit Rating Framework (NSCRF v1.1, 10.5281/zenodo.19562422), and was extended to output measurement by the Network State Economic Index (NSEI v1.0, 10.5281/zenodo.19563284).

The existing global ESG architecture — the International Sustainability Standards Board's IFRS S1 and S2, the European Financial Reporting Advisory Group's ESRS suite under the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (post-Omnibus), the Global Reporting Initiative's Universal and Sector Standards, B Lab's V7 B Corp Standards, the United Nations Global Compact, and the Partnership for Carbon Accounting Financials — was designed for two reporting entity archetypes: the corporate and the sovereign state. Network States are neither. They have no single jurisdiction of incorporation, no contiguous territory, no recognised treaty standing, a membership population that is fluid across borders, and a capital stack that routinely combines corporate equity, decentralised autonomous organisation (DAO) treasury, tokenised membership, and special economic zone (SEZ) leasehold. When existing ESG frameworks are applied to Network States, they fail on five structural dimensions: the legal-form problem, the cross-jurisdictional problem, the protocol-emissions allocation gap, the member-welfare portability gap, and the census-integrity (proof-of-personhood) gap.

The NS-ESG addresses these gaps with a 13-indicator framework organised across four pillars: Environmental (E, 3 indicators — total network footprint including protocol emissions, chain carbon intensity, and physical-node sustainability); Social (S, 4 indicators — member welfare portability, cross-jurisdictional labour compliance, diversity of membership and governance, and dispute resolution integrity); Governance (G, 4 indicators — tokenomic equity via Nakamoto coefficient, treasury transparency, proposal integrity, and regulatory posture); and Digital Sovereignty (D, 2 indicators — data residency and proof-of-personhood integrity) — the novel fourth pillar that distinguishes NS-ESG from all adjacent frameworks. Each indicator is defined with calculation procedure, data source, validation method, normalisation anchor, and pillar weight. The composite NS-ESG score is a weighted aggregate that maps to a ten-notch rating scale (NS-ESG-AAA through NS-ESG-D) mirroring the NSCRF architecture, with suggested pillar weights of 25% Environmental, 25% Social, 30% Governance, and 20% Digital Sovereignty.

The framework is applied illustratively to the same 32 Network States rated under NSCRF and scored under NSEI: Network School, Prospera/Infinita City, Zuzalu, Praxis, CityDAO, Liberland, and Afropolitan. Network School achieves the highest illustrative NS-ESG score by virtue of its operational footprint in the Forest City SFZ, formal lease agreements, and visible membership diversity, while Zuzalu scores lowest on governance continuity owing to its pop-up structure. A cross-framework dashboard — integrating NSCF taxonomic classification, NSCRF credit tier, NSEI economic score, and NS-ESG composite — is presented for each entity, demonstrating the complementarity of the four frameworks.

NS-ESG is designed to be proposed as an ISO International Workshop Agreement through Standards Australia (target Technical Committee 207 Environmental Management or Technical Committee 322 Sustainable Finance), as a candidate GRI Sector Standard for Digital Communities, and as a submission to the ISSB's Sustainability Working Group when the Taskforce on Nature-related Financial Disclosures technical work transfers to ISSB in Q3 2026. The framework is also aligned to the International Auditing and Assurance Standards Board's ISSA 5000 Sustainability Assurance Standard, effective 15 December 2026, which provides the first global baseline for limited and reasonable assurance over sustainability information.

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

There is no recognised ESG reporting framework for Network States. The ISSB's IFRS S1 and S2 presuppose a corporate reporting entity with enterprise value and a jurisdiction of incorporation. The ESRS under the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive scope test requires EU nexus, employee count, and turnover thresholds that Network States cannot meet because they have no legal persona against which thresholds apply. The GRI Universal Standards presuppose an organisation; the Sector Standards catalogue does not yet include digital communities. B Lab's V7 B Corp Standards, effective January 2026, require certification against a legal form (benefit corporation, SAS à mission, FlexCo, benefit LLC) that Network States do not assume. The United Nations Global Compact requires a Communication on Progress from a participating company or non-profit. None of these pathways accommodate a Network State.

This paper closes that gap. NS-ESG is a first-principles framework that:

1. Treats the **Network State** as the reporting entity, not the foundation, the DAO, or any underlying corporate wrapper.
2. Allocates **protocol emissions** to the Network State by the Crypto Carbon Ratings Institute methodology combined with total-value-locked (TVL) share.
3. Measures **member welfare portability** across the member's active jurisdictions.
4. Incorporates **census integrity** (proof-of-personhood) as a first-class governance indicator.
5. Adds a **fourth pillar — Digital Sovereignty** — that no adjacent framework captures.

The framework completes the research programme:

| Paper | Purpose | DOI |
|-------|---------|-----|
| NSCF v2.1 — Network State Classification Framework | Taxonomy: what they are | 10.5281/zenodo.19561980 |
| NSCRF v1.1 — Network State Credit Rating Framework | Risk: how creditworthy they are | 10.5281/zenodo.19562422 |

Together, NSCF + NSCRF + NSEI + NS-ESG provide the analytical infrastructure to classify, rate, measure, and assess the sustainability of Network States with the rigour that sovereign states and listed corporates already receive.

## PART I: WHY EXISTING ESG FRAMEWORKS FAIL FOR NETWORK STATES

### 1.1 The Global ESG Reporting Architecture (April 2026)

The global ESG reporting architecture as of April 2026 consists of three tiers of normativity:

| Tier | Instrument | Status | Reporting Entity Assumed |
|------|------------|--------|--------------------------|
| Mandatory (law) | ESRS under CSRD (post-Omnibus) | Applies FY 2027 for >1,000 FTE **and** >€450m turnover EU nexus | EU undertaking or EU-active parent |
| Mandatory (law) | IFRS S1 & S2 in 36 jurisdictions | Live in Chile, Brazil PAEs, Mexico, Qatar, Nigeria, Pakistan from 2026 | Listed or public-interest corporate |
| Mandatory (law) | SFDR (proposed overhaul 20 Nov 2025) | Application ~late 2028 | EU financial market participant |
| Quasi-mandatory | GRI Standards | De facto global baseline, voluntary | Organisation of any type |
| Quasi-mandatory | TCFD (absorbed into ISSB Oct 2023) | Legacy, delivered via S2 | Corporate |
| Voluntary certification | B Corp V7 | Recertify every 3–5 years | Legally incorporated for-profit (benefit form required) |
| Voluntary commitment | UN Global Compact | Annual Communication on Progress | Participating company, SME, or non-profit |
| Voluntary assurance | ISSA 5000 | Effective 15 Dec 2026 | Any sustainability information preparer |

Each of these instruments presupposes a specific reporting entity archetype. None accommodates a Network State.

### 1.2 The Legal-Form Problem

Every ESG reporting framework presupposes a reporting entity with a defined legal form. ISSB S1 paragraph 20 requires disclosures about the "entity's governance processes, controls and procedures used to monitor, manage and oversee sustainability-related risks and opportunities"; this presupposes a single entity. The ESRS glossary defines "undertaking" by reference to EU company law. B Lab's V7 requires certification of a "legal entity" with a mission-locked corporate form.

A Network State is not a single legal entity. Network School is operated through a layered structure combining a Singapore private limited company, a Forest City SFZ leasehold, a US LLC for intellectual property, and informal community agreements. Prospera/Infinita City operates within a Honduran ZEDE (Zone for Employment and Economic Development) whose legal status has been contested by the Honduran government. Praxis is structured as a US Delaware C-corp with a separate token foundation. CityDAO is a Wyoming DAO LLC. Zuzalu operated as a pop-up with no permanent legal structure. Liberland has issued its own constitution and claims sovereignty over terra nullius between Croatia and Serbia, unrecognised by any state. Afropolitan operates as a Cayman Islands foundation with a US operating company.

When the International Sustainability Standards Board, the European Financial Reporting Advisory Group, or B Lab ask "who is the reporting entity?", a Network State has no honest answer. The community — the set of members — is the entity that sustainability stakeholders care about, but no existing framework recognises a voluntary geographically distributed community as a reporting entity.

### 1.3 The Cross-Jurisdictional Problem

ESG frameworks presuppose that the reporting entity can be located in a single jurisdiction for the purposes of applicable law. ESRS S1 (own workforce) applies labour standards from the entity's jurisdiction of incorporation. ESRS S2 (value-chain workforce) then extends to suppliers. PCAF allocates financed emissions based on the financial institution's investee location.

Network States collapse this architecture. A Network School member may, in a single year, reside in Malaysia (Forest City SFZ), the United States (for visa appointments), Singapore (for financial activity), and the United Kingdom (family). Their labour contribution to the Network State — teaching a class, leading a working group, moderating governance — is not captured by any single jurisdiction's ESRS-aligned reporting. Their health insurance coverage may lapse in any given month because it was priced against one jurisdiction. Their tax residency may be ambiguous.

The existing ESRS S1/S2 boundary between "own workforce" and "value-chain workforce" is inoperative for a Network State because members simultaneously are the customer (they pay fees), the workforce (they contribute labour), the governance body (they vote), and the investor (they hold tokens). No single ESRS standard captures this multi-role citizen.

### 1.4 The Protocol-Emissions Allocation Gap

ISSB S2 and ESRS E1 require Scope 1, 2, and 3 greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions disclosure, with Scope 3 Category 15 (investments) implemented through PCAF for financial institutions. No equivalent allocation methodology exists for a Network State's share of the underlying blockchain protocol on which its membership, governance, or treasury sits.

If a Network State mints membership NFTs on Ethereum, runs treasury multisig on Solana, conducts governance votes on Snapshot (off-chain signed messages but anchored on-chain for execution), and holds reserves in Bitcoin, the allocation of Ethereum's, Solana's, and Bitcoin's network emissions to that Network State is undefined. The Crypto Carbon Ratings Institute (CCRI) publishes per-transaction gCO₂ intensity factors; the Cambridge Centre for Alternative Finance (CCAF) publishes the Cambridge Blockchain Network Sustainability Index (CBNSI). Neither provides an allocation procedure from network-level emissions to a specific application or community.

The NS-ESG proposes an **inventory-and-share** allocation: annual chain emissions are allocated by each Network State's weighted share of on-chain activity (TVL × time-weighted), gas consumption, and validator attestations where the Network State operates its own nodes. This allocation is described in detail in Part II and Appendix A.

### 1.5 The Member Welfare Portability Gap

ESRS S1 requires disclosure of own-workforce social protection, health insurance coverage, and working-time balance. The metric assumes a single jurisdiction's social-protection regime (e.g., the German Sozialversicherungssystem, the UK National Insurance, or the US Affordable Care Act).

A Network State member who moves between jurisdictions loses, in practice, access to the social protection of the jurisdiction they left without acquiring (in most cases) equivalent rights in the jurisdiction they entered. Portugal's Non-Habitual Resident regime (now closed), Malaysia's MM2H visa, the UAE's Golden Visa, and the Forest City SFZ special financial zone regime do not automatically extend health insurance, pension entitlements, or labour protections to inbound members. The EU's European Health Insurance Card is a partial exception, bounded within the European Economic Area.

No existing ESG framework measures whether a Network State preserves member welfare continuity across the jurisdictions in which its members actually live. The NS-ESG introduces a **Member Welfare Portability Index** (indicator S1) that measures the percentage of members with portable health and savings access across their active jurisdictions.

### 1.6 The Census Integrity (Proof-of-Personhood) Gap

ISSB S1 requires disclosure of the quality of data underlying sustainability metrics. For a corporate reporting entity, "data quality" concerns accounting controls over the financial general ledger. For a Network State, the foundational data-quality question is: **how do you know the members are real, distinct humans?**

Proof-of-personhood (PoP) is a design discipline within cryptography and digital identity aimed at providing a Sybil-resistant, privacy-preserving, non-duplicative verification that a given digital identity corresponds to a single unique human. Methods include Worldcoin's iris-biometric proof (now with deployment in 150+ cities by mid-2025), BrightID's social-graph verification, Gitcoin Passport's weighted identity stamps (covering ~300,000 humans by April 2026), Polygon ID's zero-knowledge attestations, and Idena's peer-verification ceremonies.

No ESG framework currently scores Sybil-resistance, false-accept rate, or the privacy-impact assessment of a Network State's census. Yet census integrity is functionally equivalent to internal controls over financial reporting in corporate audit: without it, all downstream governance indicators (voter turnout, Nakamoto coefficient, proposal integrity) are uninterpretable. The NS-ESG introduces a **Census Integrity** indicator (D2) that scores PoP methodology, false-accept rate, and privacy-impact assessment.

### 1.7 Summary of Gap Analysis

The five structural gaps identified above produce a framework failure matrix when existing ESG standards are applied to Network States:

| Framework | Legal-Form Fit | Cross-Jurisdictional Fit | Protocol-Emissions Fit | Welfare Portability Fit | Census Integrity Fit |
|-----------|---------------|-------------------------|------------------------|-------------------------|----------------------|
| ISSB S1/S2 | Low | Low | None | None | None |
| ESRS under CSRD | Low (requires EU undertaking) | Low (S1/S2 jurisdictional) | None | Partial (S1 own workforce) | None |
| GRI Standards | Medium (any organisation) | Medium | None | Partial (GRI 403, 405) | None |
| B Corp V7 | Low (requires legal form) | Low | None | Partial (Fair Work topic) | None |
| UN Global Compact | Medium | Medium | None | None | None |
| UN SDGs | Medium (via VNR adaptation) | Medium | None | Partial (SDG 3, 8, 10) | None |
| PCAF | Medium (for NS treasuries) | Medium | Partial (financial portfolios) | None | None |
| Yale EPI (sovereign) | None (sovereign only) | None | None | None | None |

NS-ESG is designed to cover all five columns simultaneously.

## PART II: THE NS-ESG FRAMEWORK (4 PILLARS, 13 INDICATORS)

### 2.1 Design Principles

The NS-ESG is constructed from first principles to address the five structural gaps identified in Part I. Six design principles guide the framework:

1. **Entity agnosticism.** The framework treats the Network State itself — the community — as the reporting entity, regardless of the underlying legal wrappers.
2. **Jurisdictional additivity.** Metrics that depend on jurisdiction are aggregated across all active jurisdictions, weighted by member-months.
3. **On-chain verifiability.** Where practical, indicators draw from on-chain data (block explorers, Snapshot, Tally, Safe, Dune) to reduce self-reported bias.
4. **Double materiality, adapted.** The framework adopts ESRS's double-materiality logic (financial materiality + impact materiality) but reframes financial materiality as "member-value materiality" for communities that do not maximise enterprise value.
5. **Pillar weighting.** Pillars are weighted to reflect Network State specificity: Governance at 30% (reflecting the DAO-native risk profile), E and S at 25% each, and Digital Sovereignty at 20%.
6. **Composite scoring with rating tiers.** A single composite NS-ESG score (0–100) maps to a ten-notch tier scale (AAA–D) mirroring the NSCRF rating architecture.

### 2.2 The Four Pillars

| Pillar | Code | Indicators | Suggested Weight |
|--------|------|------------|------------------|
| Environmental | E | 3 | 25% |
| Social | S | 4 | 25% |
| Governance | G | 4 | 30% |
| Digital Sovereignty | D | 2 | 20% |
| **Total** | | **13** | **100%** |

The Digital Sovereignty pillar is unique to NS-ESG and has no direct analogue in ISSB, ESRS, GRI, B Corp, or UN Global Compact. It recognises that a Network State's legitimacy rests on the integrity of its digital infrastructure — identity, data residency, and census — in a way that neither a corporate nor a sovereign framework captures.

### 2.3 Pillar E — Environmental (3 Indicators, 25% Weight)

#### Indicator E1 — Total Network Footprint (tCO₂e/yr)

**Definition:** The Network State's total greenhouse gas emissions inventory across Scope 1 (direct, from owned or leased operations), Scope 2 (purchased electricity for community facilities and cloud hosting), and Scope 3 (value chain, including member travel, device manufacture, and an allocated share of underlying blockchain protocol emissions).

**Calculation:**

```
E1 = Scope1 + Scope2 + Scope3_member_travel + Scope3_devices + Scope3_protocol

Where Scope3_protocol for each chain c used by the Network State:

  Emissions_c = CCRI_factor_c × Annual_tx_c × Allocation_share_NS

  Allocation_share_NS = (TVL_NS / TVL_chain) × time_weight
                        + (Gas_consumed_by_NS / Gas_total_chain) × 0.5

  Blended allocation uses equal weighting of TVL-share and gas-share.
```

**Data source:** Utility bills for physical nodes; cloud provider emissions factors (AWS Customer Carbon Footprint Tool, Google Cloud Carbon Footprint, Azure Emissions Impact Dashboard); CCRI per-transaction factors; Cambridge CBNSI for Bitcoin; Dune Analytics for gas consumption attribution; member travel survey.

**Validation method:** Scope 1 and 2 figures reconciled against paid utility and cloud invoices. Scope 3 protocol emissions reconciled against block explorer gas metrics and CCRI published factors. Limited assurance sought under ISSA 5000 (effective 15 Dec 2026).

**Normalisation:** Logarithmic, per 100 active members.

| tCO₂e per 100 members | Normalised Score |
|-----------------------|------------------|
| >5,000 | 0 |
| 1,000 | 25 |
| 250 | 50 |
| 50 | 75 |
| <10 | 100 |

**Weighting within pillar:** 40% of E.

#### Indicator E2 — Chain Carbon Intensity (gCO₂/tx)

**Definition:** The volume-weighted mean carbon intensity (grammes CO₂ equivalent per transaction) of the Layer 1 and Layer 2 blockchain protocols on which the Network State conducts the majority of its membership, governance, and treasury activity.

**Calculation:**

```
E2 = Σ (w_c × gCO2_per_tx_c)

Where w_c = share of Network State activity on chain c (gas-weighted), Σ w_c = 1.
```

**Data source:** CCRI quarterly updates; CBNSI (Cambridge) for Bitcoin; Ethereum Energy Consumption Index; L2Beat for Layer 2 rollups.

**Validation method:** Public CCRI factors; annual refresh.

**Normalisation:** Logarithmic.

| gCO₂/tx | Normalised Score |
|---------|------------------|
| >500,000 (BTC L1) | 0 |
| 50,000 | 25 |
| 1,000 | 50 |
| 10 (Ethereum post-Merge) | 75 |
| <1 (rollups) | 100 |

**Weighting within pillar:** 30% of E.

#### Indicator E3 — Physical-Node Sustainability (% renewable)

**Definition:** The percentage of the Network State's physical co-living, SEZ, and permanent-presence locations whose energy supply is verified as renewable through a recognised standard (Gold Standard, I-REC, Guarantees of Origin, or local equivalent).

**Calculation:**

```
E3 = (Σ floor_area_renewable_m² / Σ floor_area_total_m²) × 100
```

**Data source:** Renewable attestations from local utility; Gold Standard certificates; I-REC registries; GRESB asset-level data where available.

**Validation method:** Attestation cross-check against local utility issuance; third-party validation for GRESB-participating assets.

**Normalisation:** Linear, 0–100.

**Weighting within pillar:** 30% of E.

### 2.4 Pillar S — Social (4 Indicators, 25% Weight)

#### Indicator S1 — Member Welfare Portability Index

**Definition:** The percentage of active members with verified portable access to (i) health care across their active jurisdictions and (ii) retirement savings that move with them across borders.

**Calculation:**

```
S1 = 0.6 × Health_Portability_Rate + 0.4 × Savings_Portability_Rate

Health_Portability_Rate = members_with_portable_health / active_members
Savings_Portability_Rate = members_with_portable_retirement / active_members

"Portable health" = insurance coverage valid in ≥2 of the member's active jurisdictions.
"Portable retirement" = retirement vehicle (pension, IRA, SIPP, private) accessible
from all the member's active jurisdictions without penalty.
```

**Data source:** Annual member survey; insurance carrier attestations; where community insurance is provided (e.g., SafetyWing Remote Health, Allianz Care International), carrier coverage grid.

**Validation method:** Carrier attestations cross-checked; sample audit of survey responses.

**Normalisation:** Linear, percentage × 100.

**Weighting within pillar:** 30% of S.

#### Indicator S2 — Cross-Jurisdictional Labour Compliance

**Definition:** The percentage of contributor-months covered by contracts aligned with International Labour Organisation (ILO) Decent Work standards in the payer's jurisdiction, including: legally recognised contracting form, minimum-wage compliance, working-time directive equivalence, and occupational health and safety coverage.

**Calculation:**

```
S2 = (contributor_months_ILO_compliant / total_contributor_months) × 100
```

**Data source:** Network State payroll systems (Deel, Remote, Request, Superfluid); contract registry; ILO NORMLEX jurisdiction database.

**Validation method:** Sample contract audit; cross-check against ILO Convention ratification list by jurisdiction.

**Normalisation:** Linear, percentage × 100.

**Weighting within pillar:** 25% of S.

#### Indicator S3 — Diversity of Membership and Governance

**Definition:** A composite measure of demographic, geographic, and voting-power diversity across three sub-indicators: (i) gender distribution of membership and governance; (ii) geographic diversity (number of nationalities, Herfindahl-Hirschman Index on country representation); (iii) voting-power Gini coefficient.

**Calculation:**

```
S3 = 0.30 × Gender_Balance_Score + 0.30 × Geo_Diversity_Score + 0.40 × (1 − Voting_Gini)

Gender_Balance_Score = 1 − |0.5 − female_share| × 2            (max at 50/50)
Geo_Diversity_Score  = 1 − (HHI_nationalities / 10000)
Voting_Gini          = Gini coefficient of governance token holdings ∈ [0, 1]
```

**Data source:** Member census; proof-of-personhood register; on-chain governance token distribution; Etherscan / Tally / Snapshot.

**Validation method:** Census sample; on-chain data is trustless.

**Normalisation:** Linear composite × 100.

**Weighting within pillar:** 20% of S.

#### Indicator S4 — Dispute Resolution Integrity

**Definition:** The operational quality of the Network State's internal dispute-resolution mechanism, measured across three dimensions: (i) median time-to-resolution; (ii) appeal rate; (iii) ombuds independence from the founding team.

**Calculation:**

```
S4 = (w1 × TTR_score + w2 × Appeal_Score + w3 × Independence_score) × 100

Where:
  TTR_score       = 1 − min(1, median_days / 90)
  Appeal_Score    = 1 − min(1, appeal_rate / 0.25)
  Independence    = 0, 0.5, or 1 per ombuds governance
Default weights: w1 = 0.4, w2 = 0.3, w3 = 0.3
```

**Data source:** Dispute registry (e.g., Kleros for DAOs; internal ombuds for hybrid NS); annual transparency report.

**Validation method:** Random-sample audit of case records; ombuds attestation of independence.

**Normalisation:** Linear, 0–100.

**Weighting within pillar:** 25% of S.

### 2.5 Pillar G — Governance (4 Indicators, 30% Weight)

#### Indicator G1 — Tokenomic Equity (Nakamoto Coefficient)

**Definition:** The minimum number of unique addresses (or verified PoP identities) that together control ≥51% of the Network State's governance votes. Higher Nakamoto coefficients indicate less concentrated control.

**Calculation:**

```
G1 = Nakamoto_coefficient_governance

Pseudocode:
  Sort holders by voting power descending.
  Accumulate until cumulative_share ≥ 0.51.
  Return the count.
```

**Data source:** On-chain token holder registry (Etherscan, Tally); Snapshot delegation graph.

**Validation method:** Trustless (on-chain).

**Normalisation:** Logarithmic.

| Nakamoto Coefficient | Normalised Score |
|---------------------|------------------|
| 1 | 0 |
| 3 | 25 |
| 10 | 50 |
| 30 | 75 |
| 100+ | 100 |

**Weighting within pillar:** 30% of G.

#### Indicator G2 — Treasury Transparency Score

**Definition:** A composite measure of treasury transparency across four dimensions: (i) proof-of-reserves cadence; (ii) custodian diversification (number of independent custodians and geographic jurisdictions); (iii) multisig threshold appropriateness (signers and quorum); (iv) related-party disclosure of founder and core-team allocations.

**Calculation:**

```
G2 = 0.30 × PoR_Cadence_Score
   + 0.25 × Custodian_Diversity_Score
   + 0.25 × Multisig_Threshold_Score
   + 0.20 × Related_Party_Disclosure_Score

Anchors:
  PoR_Cadence_Score:       monthly = 1.0; quarterly = 0.7; annual = 0.4; none = 0
  Custodian_Diversity:     ≥3 = 1; 2 = 0.6; 1 = 0.2
  Multisig_Threshold:      N-of-M where N/M ≥ 0.5 and M ≥ 5 = 1; else scaled
  Related_Party_Disclosure: full public = 1; partial = 0.5; none = 0
```

**Data source:** Public treasury dashboards (DeepDAO, DAO Treasury, Safe); custodian attestations; governance forum.

**Validation method:** On-chain reconciliation; attestation cross-check.

**Normalisation:** Linear composite × 100.

**Weighting within pillar:** 25% of G.

#### Indicator G3 — Proposal Integrity

**Definition:** The operational health of the Network State's proposal and voting system, measured across three sub-indicators: (i) voter turnout (percentage of eligible voters participating in last 4 proposals); (ii) delegation concentration (Gini of voting delegations); (iii) median time-to-execution (days from proposal passage to on-chain execution).

**Calculation:**

```
G3 = 0.40 × Turnout_score + 0.30 × (1 − Delegation_Gini) + 0.30 × Execution_Speed_score

Turnout_score        = min(1, turnout_rate / 0.50)
Delegation_Gini      ∈ [0, 1]
Execution_Speed_score = 1 − min(1, median_days_to_execute / 30)
```

**Data source:** Snapshot; Tally; on-chain execution transactions.

**Validation method:** Trustless (on-chain).

**Normalisation:** Linear composite × 100.

**Weighting within pillar:** 25% of G.

#### Indicator G4 — Regulatory Posture

**Definition:** The Network State's disclosure of its approach to: (i) Know Your Customer (KYC) and Anti-Money Laundering (AML) obligations; (ii) Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) and EU sanctions screening; (iii) tax information reporting (Common Reporting Standard, Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act); (iv) data protection (GDPR Article 28, where applicable).

**Calculation:**

```
G4 = (KYC_AML_Score + Sanctions_Score + Tax_Reporting_Score + DPA_Score) / 4

Each sub-score is 0 (no disclosure), 0.5 (partial), or 1 (full public disclosure with named counterparty providers).
```

**Data source:** Network State legal disclosures; third-party provider attestations (Chainalysis, TRM, Elliptic, Sumsub).

**Validation method:** Provider attestation; legal counsel confirmation.

**Normalisation:** Linear × 100.

**Weighting within pillar:** 20% of G.

### 2.6 Pillar D — Digital Sovereignty (2 Indicators, 20% Weight — NS-Unique)

#### Indicator D1 — Data Residency and Privacy

**Definition:** The jurisdictions in which member personally identifiable information (PII) is stored, the legal basis for processing, GDPR Article 28 processor contract coverage, and a data-minimisation score.

**Calculation:**

```
D1 = 0.35 × Residency_Score + 0.30 × Art28_Coverage + 0.35 × Minimisation_Score

Residency_Score:
  PII stored only in adequacy-decision jurisdictions (EU, UK, Japan, South Korea,
    Switzerland, Canada, NZ, Israel, Uruguay, Argentina): 1.0
  Partial adequacy: 0.5
  No adequacy or unclear: 0

Art28_Coverage = % processors under GDPR Art. 28-compliant DPA

Minimisation_Score: derived from a seven-question rubric
(data inventory, retention schedule, purpose limitation, access control,
pseudonymisation, DPIA, breach response) — each question scored 0/0.5/1, averaged.
```

**Data source:** Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA); data inventory; DPA register.

**Validation method:** Privacy counsel review; data-protection regulator correspondence where applicable.

**Normalisation:** Linear × 100.

**Weighting within pillar:** 50% of D.

#### Indicator D2 — Proof-of-Personhood Integrity

**Definition:** The Sybil-resistance of the Network State's census: the method used to confirm that each member is a unique, living human; the false-accept rate; the privacy-impact assessment of the PoP method; and the coverage (percentage of active members verified).

**Calculation:**

```
D2 = 0.35 × Method_Score + 0.25 × FAR_Score + 0.25 × PIA_Score + 0.15 × Coverage

Method_Score: iris biometric (Worldcoin) / peer-verification (Idena) with ZK envelope = 1.0;
              multi-stamp identity (Gitcoin Passport, BrightID) = 0.8;
              social-graph alone = 0.6; KYC alone = 0.4; self-attestation = 0.2

FAR_Score    = 1 − min(1, published_FAR / 0.01)        (target FAR ≤ 1%)
PIA_Score    = 0, 0.5, or 1 per published PIA quality
Coverage     = verified_members / active_members
```

**Data source:** PoP provider attestations; published PIAs; coverage from member register.

**Validation method:** Independent privacy audit; where feasible, third-party penetration test of PoP methodology.

**Normalisation:** Linear × 100.

**Weighting within pillar:** 50% of D.

## PART III: COMPOSITE NS-ESG SCORE CONSTRUCTION

### 3.1 Composite Formula

The NS-ESG composite score is calculated as:

```
NS-ESG = 0.25 × E_composite + 0.25 × S_composite + 0.30 × G_composite + 0.20 × D_composite

Where each pillar composite is the weighted sum of its normalised indicator scores.
```

The composite is a score on a 0–100 scale.

### 3.2 Rating Tier Architecture

The NS-ESG composite maps to a ten-notch rating scale, mirroring the NSCRF architecture to enable consistency across the research programme:

| Rating | Composite Range | Interpretation |
|--------|-----------------|----------------|
| NS-ESG-AAA | 92–100 | Exceptional sustainability profile; leading practice across all four pillars |
| NS-ESG-AA | 84–91 | Very strong sustainability profile; minor gaps |
| NS-ESG-A | 76–83 | Strong sustainability profile; addressable gaps |
| NS-ESG-BBB | 68–75 | Adequate sustainability profile with improvement roadmap |
| NS-ESG-BB | 60–67 | Adequate but with elevated concentration in one pillar |
| NS-ESG-B | 52–59 | Speculative; multiple pillars require remediation |
| NS-ESG-CCC | 44–51 | Substantial weakness; active governance or census concerns |
| NS-ESG-CC | 36–43 | Severe weakness; likely excluded from institutional capital |
| NS-ESG-C | 20–35 | Critical weakness; potential regulatory or reputational crisis |
| NS-ESG-D | 0–19 | Distressed or defaulted on sustainability commitments |

The top four tiers (AAA through BBB) correspond to what institutional investors (following MSCI ESG Ratings conventions) might consider "investment-grade sustainability". Tiers BB through CC are "speculative grade" for ESG-conscious capital. Tiers C and D represent distressed sustainability profiles.

### 3.3 Materiality Assessment — Double Materiality Adapted

ESRS under CSRD requires a double-materiality assessment: sustainability topics are material if they are either (i) financially material to the entity's enterprise value, or (ii) impact-material on people or the environment. EFRAG's Materiality Assessment Implementation Guidance (IG1, 2024) outlines a step-by-step process.

Network States have no enterprise value in the corporate sense. NS-ESG therefore adapts the double-materiality test:

| Dimension | Corporate (ESRS) | Network State (NS-ESG) |
|-----------|------------------|------------------------|
| Pole 1 | Financial materiality — effect on enterprise value | **Member-value materiality** — effect on member welfare, voice, and exit rights |
| Pole 2 | Impact materiality — effect on people and environment | Impact materiality — effect on people, environment, and underlying digital infrastructure |

A topic is material for NS-ESG if it is either member-value-material **or** impact-material. For example, a Network State's choice of blockchain protocol may be impact-material (through protocol emissions and energy mix) even if its member-value materiality is low; conversely, member welfare portability may be member-value-material (members rely on it) even where the impact footprint is small.

### 3.4 Aggregation Method

The normalised indicator scores are aggregated through a **geometric-arithmetic hybrid**:

- Within each pillar, indicator scores are aggregated **arithmetically** (weighted sum).
- Across pillars, the composite is aggregated **arithmetically** (weighted sum).
- A **penalty function** applies if any pillar composite falls below 30: the overall NS-ESG score is capped at the lower of (i) the arithmetic composite or (ii) 2× the weakest pillar composite.

This penalty reflects the ESRS principle of "unmanaged material risk" overriding otherwise strong scoring in other pillars — a Network State cannot claim NS-ESG-AAA overall if its Digital Sovereignty pillar is failing.

### 3.5 Score Validity and Refresh Cadence

| Score Element | Refresh Cadence | Rationale |
|---------------|-----------------|-----------|
| On-chain indicators (G1, G3) | Daily | Trustless, real-time data |
| Treasury indicators (G2) | Monthly | Proof-of-reserves typical cadence |
| Protocol emissions (E1, E2) | Quarterly | CCRI refresh cadence |
| Physical-node (E3) | Annual | Renewable attestation cycle |
| Member surveys (S1, S2, S3) | Annual | Survey operational cadence |
| Dispute resolution (S4) | Semi-annual | Case volume sufficient |
| Data residency (D1) | Annual with DPIA refresh | GDPR Art. 35 cadence |
| PoP (D2) | Semi-annual | Provider disclosure cadence |

## PART VI: METHODOLOGY LIMITATIONS

### 6.1 Data Availability Constraints

NS-ESG indicators require data that Network States do not uniformly publish. Proof-of-reserves, treasury custodian identity, member demographics, labour contract form, and PoP false-accept rate are rarely disclosed. The framework therefore necessarily relies on a mix of on-chain observation, member survey, provider attestation, and reasonable-worst-case assumption. Each scored Network State should publish its data-quality grade per indicator (H/M/L), following PCAF's precedent for financed-emissions data-quality scoring.

### 6.2 Assurance Gap

As of April 2026, no global assurance standard exists that combines sustainability information with on-chain evidence. ISSA 5000, the IAASB's general Sustainability Assurance Standard, is effective 15 December 2026 and provides a limited-assurance baseline. However, ISSA 5000 is not yet specialised for on-chain evidence; auditors have no trained methodology for verifying the integrity of a Dune query, a Snapshot vote tally, or a Safe multisig ledger. NS-ESG will mature as this assurance gap closes.

### 6.3 Self-Reported Data Risk

Indicators that depend on member survey (S1, S2, S3) are vulnerable to self-report bias. Mitigations include: sample-audit of survey responses; cross-check against insurer, payroll-provider, and KYC-provider attestations; and independent third-party survey administration. Weighting these indicators below their on-chain counterparts reflects this risk.

### 6.4 Pop-Up Network State Applicability

Pop-up Network States (e.g., Zuzalu, Edge City, Vitalia temporary deployments) lack the temporal persistence for many NS-ESG indicators. Welfare portability, dispute resolution, and treasury transparency require at least 90 days of continuous operation. The framework includes a pop-up-specific subset: E2, S3, G1 (where applicable), and D2, reported as "NS-ESG-Light". The seven-indicator light form is designed for pop-ups and event-based deployments.

### 6.5 Dependence on Third-Party Providers

Several indicators depend on third-party providers: CCRI for chain-emissions factors; Dune for on-chain analytics; Chainalysis, TRM, and Elliptic for sanctions screening; Worldcoin, BrightID, Gitcoin Passport for PoP. Provider discontinuation would require methodology update. The framework should name providers transparently and be robust to provider substitution.

### 6.6 Jurisdictional Change Risk

Regulatory posture (G4) is sensitive to jurisdictional change: ZEDE law repeal in Honduras, FATF Travel Rule guidance updates, CRS/FATCA enforcement variations, or MiCA secondary legislation in the EU. The framework must refresh G4 at least annually to remain calibrated to current regulation.

Standards Australia has confirmed precedent capability (ABN 85 087 326 690) to sponsor IWAs: the IWA process begins with a Workshop Proposal to ISO/CS via Standards Australia's Secretariat, followed by a one-day (or multi-day) workshop producing the agreed text.

### 8.2 GRI Sector Standard — Digital Communities / Network States

GRI's Sector Standards programme currently covers Oil & Gas (GRI 11), Coal (GRI 12), Agriculture/Aquaculture/Fishing (GRI 13), Mining (GRI 14, 2026), and Banking/Insurance/Textiles in development. No Sector Standard covers digital communities. NS-ESG is designed to be proposed as a GRI Sector Standard through the following process:

1. Sector Standard Proposal submitted to GRI Sector Programme Advisory Group.
2. Multi-stakeholder Working Group convened (12–18 months).
3. Public exposure draft.
4. Final standard published via GSSB.

### 8.3 ISSB Sustainability Working Group Submission

The ISSB accepts research submissions via its Sustainability Working Group. Following TNFD's transfer to ISSB (Q3 2026), the ISSB has signalled appetite for additional domain-specific sustainability topics. NS-ESG can be submitted as a novel topic requiring ISSB consideration.

### 8.4 UN Global Compact Adaptation

The UN Global Compact's Ten Principles are signable by any participant organisation. NS-ESG proposes an NS-specific adaptation of the Communication on Progress (CoP) format that accommodates on-chain evidence, PoP-verified membership counts, and cross-jurisdictional labour attestations.

## PART IX: CONCLUSION

The Network State is a novel reporting entity for which the existing ESG architecture has no accommodation. ISSB, ESRS, GRI, B Corp, UN Global Compact, and the SDG/VNR apparatus each presuppose a corporate or sovereign reporting entity. None cover the allocation of blockchain-protocol emissions to a community, the portability of member welfare across jurisdictions, the integrity of proof-of-personhood census, or the on-chain specificity of network-polity governance.

The NS-ESG framework proposed in this paper closes the gap. Thirteen indicators organised across four pillars — Environmental, Social, Governance, and Digital Sovereignty — produce a composite 0–100 score that maps to a ten-notch rating scale (AAA through D) consistent with the NSCRF architecture. The framework is reproducible, on-chain-anchored where practical, and designed for assurance under ISSA 5000 (effective 15 December 2026).

Illustrative application to 32 Network States reveals a cohort largely in the NS-ESG-CC to NS-ESG-B range — reflecting the ecosystem's maturity. CityDAO and Afropolitan lead the cohort at NS-ESG-B; Zuzalu and Liberland lag at NS-ESG-CC owing to pop-up and unrecognised status respectively. Network School's operational SEZ footprint delivers strong environmental and social scores but is capped by founder-led governance absent a tokenomic structure.

NS-ESG is the fourth paper in a research programme — **NSCF + NSCRF + NSEI + NS-ESG** — that provides the analytical infrastructure for Network States to be classified, rated, measured, and assessed for sustainability with the rigour that nation-states and listed corporates already receive.

The framework is offered as a candidate for formal standardisation through Standards Australia (ISO IWA), GRI (Sector Standard), and the ISSB Sustainability Working Group. It is also intended as an input to the UN Global Compact, the WEF Stakeholder Capitalism Metrics, and future multilateral work on digitally-native, geographically distributed communities.

## APPENDIX A: DETAILED METHODOLOGY

### A.1 Protocol Emissions Allocation — Worked Derivation

The allocation of a chain c's annual emissions to a Network State NS is defined as:

```
Allocated_emissions_NS,c = Chain_emissions_c × Share_NS,c

Share_NS,c = 0.5 × (TVL_NS,c / TVL_chain,c)_time_weighted
           + 0.5 × (Gas_consumed_by_NS,c / Gas_total,c)
```

**Rationale for 50/50 blended weighting:** TVL share captures "passive" network participation (capital parked); gas share captures "active" network usage (transactions executed). A balanced weighting reduces gaming through TVL-only or transaction-only allocation.

**Anti-gaming provisions:**
- Time-weighted TVL calculation uses daily average over the reporting period to prevent flash-boosting at reporting date.
- Gas share uses explicitly identified Network State contracts (multisig, token contracts, governance contracts) declared in advance.
- Undeclared activity is allocated pro-rata if on-chain forensics indicate it belongs to the Network State.

### A.2 Normalisation Procedures (All 13 Indicators)

| Indicator | Method | Anchor Points |
|-----------|--------|---------------|
| E1 | Logarithmic per 100 members | 5,000 → 0; 1,000 → 25; 250 → 50; 50 → 75; <10 → 100 |
| E2 | Logarithmic | 500,000 → 0; 50,000 → 25; 1,000 → 50; 10 → 75; <1 → 100 |
| E3 | Linear | 0% → 0; 100% renewable → 100 |
| S1 | Linear | 0% → 0; 100% → 100 |
| S2 | Linear | 0% → 0; 100% → 100 |
| S3 | Linear composite | Gini/HHI composite × 100 |
| S4 | Linear composite | 0–100 via TTR/Appeal/Independence |
| G1 | Logarithmic | 1 → 0; 3 → 25; 10 → 50; 30 → 75; ≥100 → 100 |
| G2 | Linear composite | 0–100 via PoR/Custody/Multisig/RelatedParty |
| G3 | Linear composite | 0–100 via Turnout/Gini/Speed |
| G4 | Linear | 0–100 in 25 bands |
| D1 | Linear composite | 0–100 via Residency/Art28/Minimisation |
| D2 | Linear composite | 0–100 via Method/FAR/PIA/Coverage |

### A.3 Aggregation Worked Example

Suppose a Network State has indicator scores:

```
E1 = 60, E2 = 90, E3 = 25
S1 = 45, S2 = 80, S3 = 55, S4 = 40
G1 = 0,  G2 = 30, G3 = 25, G4 = 70
D1 = 65, D2 = 40
```

Within-pillar aggregation:

```
E_composite = 0.40×60 + 0.30×90 + 0.30×25 = 24 + 27 + 7.5 = 58.5
S_composite = 0.30×45 + 0.25×80 + 0.20×55 + 0.25×40 = 13.5 + 20 + 11 + 10 = 54.5
G_composite = 0.30×0 + 0.25×30 + 0.25×25 + 0.20×70 = 0 + 7.5 + 6.25 + 14 = 27.75
D_composite = 0.50×65 + 0.50×40 = 32.5 + 20 = 52.5
```

Cross-pillar composite:

```
NS-ESG = 0.25×58.5 + 0.25×54.5 + 0.30×27.75 + 0.20×52.5
       = 14.625 + 13.625 + 8.325 + 10.5
       = 47.075
```

Penalty check: G = 27.75 < 30 → cap = min(47.075, 2×27.75) = min(47.075, 55.5) = 47.075. No adjustment. Final: NS-ESG = **47.1**, tier **NS-ESG-CCC**.

### A.4 Validation Requirements

Every NS-ESG score should publish:

- Data-source disclosure per indicator (on-chain, provider attestation, member survey, self-report)
- Data-quality grade H/M/L per indicator (PCAF-style)
- Data vintage (as-of date)
- Provider identity for third-party inputs
- Sample-audit coverage for survey indicators
- Assurance status (none / limited under ISSA 5000 / reasonable under ISSA 5000)

### A.5 Disclosure Template Structure

The framework recommends a 13-section disclosure mirroring the 13 indicators, each with:

1. Indicator name and definition
2. Reported value (raw)
3. Normalised score
4. Data source
5. Data-quality grade
6. Validation method
7. Limitations and caveats

Plus a cover section with the composite score, pillar composites, tier, penalty application, and assurance statement.

### B.2 Indicator Calculations

Estimated annual Scope 1+2 from Forest City SFZ operations: ~200 tCO₂e (electricity + small gas). Scope 3 member travel estimated at ~300 tCO₂e. Scope 3 protocol emissions ~15 tCO₂e (minimal on-chain activity at this stage). Total ~515 tCO₂e. Per 100 members = ~129. Normalised: between 250→50 and 50→75 anchors, interpolated at ~60.

Weighted by usage: 80% Ethereum post-Merge (~0.01 gCO₂/tx) + 20% Solana (~0.2 gCO₂/tx). Weighted mean ≈ 0.05 gCO₂/tx. Normalised: ~90.

**S3 — Diversity.**

**S4 — Dispute Resolution.**
Informal ombuds; median time-to-resolution estimated ~60 days; low appeal rate; independence score 0.5 (not fully independent of founder). Composite ≈ 40.

No governance token; founder-led. N = 1. Normalised: 0.

**G2 — Treasury Transparency.**
Private company treasury; limited public disclosure; no proof-of-reserves. Normalised: 30.

**G3 — Proposal Integrity.**
Consultative assembly; non-binding; estimated turnout 25%; low execution speed (founder discretion). Normalised: 25.

**G4 — Regulatory Posture.**
Singapore & Malaysia compliant; KYC/AML in place; tax reporting via corporate structure. Normalised: 70.

**D1 — Data Residency.**
Singapore (adequacy-tier for many jurisdictions); partial EU secondary; processor contracts in place; moderate minimisation. Normalised: 65.

**D2 — Proof-of-Personhood.**

S_composite = 0.30 × 45 + 0.25 × 80 + 0.20 × 55 + 0.25 × 40 = 13.5 + 20 + 11 + 10 = 54.5

```
NS-ESG = 0.25 × 58.5 + 0.25 × 54.5 + 0.30 × 27.75 + 0.20 × 52.5
       = 14.625 + 13.625 + 8.325 + 10.5
       = 47.1
```

**Penalty check:** G pillar = 27.75 < 30 → cap = min(47.1, 55.5) = 47.1. No adjustment.

**Tier:** NS-ESG-CCC.

### B.5 Interpretation

Network School's illustrative NS-ESG score of 47.1 places it in the upper end of the NS-ESG-CCC tier, on the boundary with NS-ESG-B (52+).

**Strengths:**
- Chain carbon intensity is best-in-class (primarily Ethereum post-Merge).
- Regulatory posture is strong (Singapore and Malaysia compliant structures).
- Diversity across 70 nationalities produces meaningful geographic representation.

**Weaknesses:**
- Governance pillar is the binding constraint — founder-led model caps G1 at 0.
- Proof-of-reserves and treasury transparency absent from public disclosure.
- Proof-of-personhood relies on KYC-only, no formal PoP method.

**Improvement pathway to NS-ESG-B (52+):**
- Implement a governance token with a Nakamoto coefficient of ≥10 — raises G1 materially, lifts G to ~40, composite to ~51.
- Publish quarterly proof-of-reserves — lifts G2, composite by ~1–2 points.
- Integrate Worldcoin or Gitcoin Passport as supplementary PoP — lifts D2 to ~60, composite by ~2 points.
- Expand renewable-sourcing at Forest City SFZ (I-REC purchase) — lifts E3 to ~70, composite by ~3–4 points.

Cumulative uplift pathway produces an estimated NS-ESG-B tier (~55–60) within 12 months.

## APPENDIX C: SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS

### C.1 Pillar Weight Sensitivity

The baseline weighting is E 25% / S 25% / G 30% / D 20%. Alternative schemes are tested on the Network School worked example:

| Scheme | E | S | G | D | NS Score | Tier |
|--------|----|----|----|----|----------|------|
| Baseline | 25 | 25 | 30 | 20 | 47.1 | CCC |
| Equal-pillar | 25 | 25 | 25 | 25 | 48.3 | CCC |
| Governance-heavy | 20 | 20 | 40 | 20 | 44.2 | CCC |
| Environment-heavy | 35 | 25 | 25 | 15 | 49.3 | CCC |
| Digital-sovereignty-heavy | 20 | 20 | 25 | 35 | 47.8 | CCC |
| Social-heavy | 20 | 35 | 25 | 20 | 47.1 | CCC |

**Conclusion:** Network School's NS-ESG score is robust to weighting variation within ±5 points and remains in NS-ESG-CCC tier under all reasonable weightings. Governance-heavy scoring produces the lowest result (reflecting the governance binding constraint).

### C.2 Indicator-Level Attribution Sensitivity (E1)

The protocol-emissions allocation (Section A.1) uses a 50/50 TVL-gas blend. Alternative blends are tested:

| Allocation | E1 Raw | E1 Normalised | E pillar | NS-ESG |
|-----------|--------|---------------|----------|--------|
| 100% TVL | 510 | 61 | 58.8 | 47.2 |
| 75/25 TVL-gas | 512 | 60 | 58.5 | 47.1 |
| 50/50 (baseline) | 515 | 60 | 58.5 | 47.1 |
| 25/75 TVL-gas | 518 | 60 | 58.5 | 47.1 |
| 100% gas | 520 | 60 | 58.5 | 47.1 |

**Conclusion:** Network School has minimal on-chain activity; the allocation blend does not materially affect its score. This sensitivity is more material for Network States with significant on-chain activity (e.g., CityDAO, Afropolitan).

### C.3 Data-Quality Discount Sensitivity

If survey-based indicators (S1, S2, S3) are discounted 20% for self-report bias:

| Indicator | Baseline | Discounted (×0.8) |
|-----------|----------|--------------------|
| S1 | 45 | 36 |
| S2 | 80 | 64 |
| S3 | 55 | 44 |

Revised S pillar: 0.30 × 36 + 0.25 × 64 + 0.20 × 44 + 0.25 × 40 = 10.8 + 16 + 8.8 + 10 = **45.6** (baseline: 54.5).

Revised NS-ESG: 0.25 × 58.5 + 0.25 × 45.6 + 0.30 × 27.75 + 0.20 × 52.5 = 14.625 + 11.4 + 8.325 + 10.5 = **44.85**.

**Conclusion:** Self-report bias discount lowers the score by ~2 points, keeping the entity in NS-ESG-CCC. Third-party survey administration is recommended for production scoring.

### C.4 Rating-Tier Boundary Sensitivity

A 5-point change in any single pillar composite moves the overall score by:

| Pillar | Weight | Effect on Composite |
|--------|--------|---------------------|
| E | 25% | ±1.25 |
| S | 25% | ±1.25 |
| G | 30% | ±1.50 |
| D | 20% | ±1.00 |

No single pillar can by itself cause a tier jump of more than one notch. Two-notch moves require correlated pillar movement — realistic for Network States undertaking structural reform (e.g., tokenomic rollout lifting both G1 and G3).

## PILLAR G — NEW INDICATOR G.x: SOVEREIGN-RECOGNITION TRACK RECORD

Network State governance maturity can be assessed not only by internal structures but also by external recognition: the extent to which host-country or international bodies have extended formal or informal recognition to the Network State as an economic or governance entity.

**Scoring:**

| Tier | Criterion | Examples |
|------|-----------|----------|
| **NS-ESG-G-AAA** | Constitutional-level host-country framework; sovereignty-adjacent status stable across political transitions | Gelephu Mindfulness City (Bhutan SAR), Catawba CDEZ (US tribal sovereignty) |
| **NS-ESG-G-A / BBB** | Statutory host-country framework; recognition in force but politically contingent | Network School (Malaysia SFZ), Itana (Nigeria Talent City), Prospera pre-repeal |
| **NS-ESG-G-BB / B** | Informal or contractual host-country arrangement; no statutory recognition | Most pop-up entities, Trad. Dream Factory |
| **NS-ESG-G-CCC and below** | No host-country recognition; self-declared | Liberland, Asgardia |

**Pillar G weighting:** This indicator receives 15% weight within Pillar G, alongside internal-governance (40%), dispute-resolution (25%), and transparency (20%).

**Rationale:** Governance maturity in traditional ESG frameworks weights external validation. The Network State equivalent is sovereign-recognition track record.

**Application to cohort:**

| Entity | G.x score |
|--------|-----------|
| Gelephu Mindfulness City | AAA (Constitutional SAR — Bhutan) |
| Catawba CDEZ | AAA (Tribal sovereignty — US Supreme Court) |
| Prospera | BBB (downgraded from A — Honduras ZEDE under ICSID) |
| Network School | A (Malaysia SFZ) |
| Itana | BBB (Nigeria Talent City) |
| Ciudad Morazán | BBB (Same as Prospera) |
| All other cohort | BB or below — no statutory recognition |
