# Measuring the Economic Output of Network States
## Research Brief for KOL Content Series
### Compiled: 5 April 2026

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Network States represent an emerging form of political and economic organization that exists between companies and countries. Founded on Balaji Srinivasan's 2022 framework, these cloud-first communities are growing rapidly -- approximately 117 startup societies are now tracked globally, with combined funding exceeding $1 billion. Yet no established framework exists to measure their economic output. Traditional GDP cannot capture the value created by geographically distributed, digitally-native communities whose members span dozens of countries.

This brief proposes the first comprehensive measurement framework for Network State economics. It draws on existing metrics from network economics (Metcalfe's Law), DAO governance, startup ecosystem analysis, special economic zone literature, and digital nomad impact studies to construct a novel set of indicators: the **Network State Economic Index (NSEI)**. The framework is designed to make invisible economic value visible and comparable to traditional jurisdictions.

## 1. WHAT ARE NETWORK STATES?

### 1.1 Balaji Srinivasan's Definition

The canonical one-line definition from *The Network State: How To Start a New Country* (2022):

> "A network state is a highly aligned online community with a capacity for collective action that crowdfunds territory around the world and eventually gains diplomatic recognition from pre-existing states."

The longer definition expands this into a social network with:
- A moral innovation (the "one commandment" -- a single unifying principle)
- A sense of national consciousness
- A recognized founder
- A capacity for collective action
- An in-person level of civility
- An integrated cryptocurrency
- A consensual government limited by a social smart contract
- An archipelago of crowdfunded physical territories
- A virtual capital
- An on-chain census that proves a large enough population, income, and real-estate footprint to attain diplomatic recognition

Source: https://thenetworkstate.com/the-network-state-in-one-sentence

### 1.2 The Progression Framework

Srinivasan describes a staged development path:

1. **Startup Society** -- A small online community organized around a shared principle
2. **Network Union** -- The community develops collective bargaining power and capacity for coordinated action
3. **Network Archipelago** -- The community crowdfunds physical territory (not necessarily contiguous) across multiple locations
4. **Network State** -- The community achieves sufficient population, income, and real-estate footprint to gain diplomatic recognition

At each stage, the community proves legitimacy through real-time, on-chain dashboards displaying population size, financial reserves, square meters controlled, and compliance with the founding principle.

Source: https://thenetworkstate.com/

### 1.3 Key Characteristics

| Characteristic | Description |
|---|---|
| **Cloud-first** | The community forms online before acquiring physical territory |
| **Moral innovation** | Organized around a singular purpose (health, longevity, technology, etc.) |
| **Collective action** | Members coordinate to achieve shared economic and political goals |
| **Crowdfunded territory** | Land and buildings are collectively purchased, forming an archipelago |
| **On-chain census** | Transparent, cryptographically verified metrics of population and wealth |
| **Diplomatic recognition** | The ultimate goal: recognition by existing nation-states |

### 1.4 How Network States Differ from Adjacent Concepts

| Feature | Network State | Nation-State | DAO | Charter City | SEZ | Digital Nomad Community |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Origin** | Online community | Historical territory | Smart contract | Government charter | Government decree | Organic gathering |
| **Territory** | Archipelago (distributed) | Contiguous | None (virtual) | Single location | Single zone | None (transient) |
| **Governance** | Social smart contract | Constitution/law | Token voting | Bespoke charter | National law + exemptions | Informal |
| **Membership** | Voluntary, global | Citizenship by birth/naturalization | Token holding | Residency | Business registration | Self-identification |
| **Economic model** | Integrated crypto + physical economy | Tax-based GDP | Treasury + token value | Tax incentives | Export/FDI focused | Individual earning |
| **Sovereignty goal** | Diplomatic recognition | Already sovereign | None | Autonomy within state | Limited fiscal autonomy | None |
| **Census** | On-chain, real-time | Periodic (5-10 years) | Wallet addresses | Municipal records | Business registrations | None |
| **Population** | 100s-1000s (current) | Millions-billions | Token holders (often thousands) | Thousands-millions | Workers (often transient) | Millions (distributed) |

**Key distinctions:**
- **vs. DAOs**: Network States require physical territory and in-person community; DAOs are purely virtual governance structures. Network States aim for sovereignty; DAOs aim for coordination.
- **vs. Charter Cities**: Charter Cities receive their charter from an existing government and operate within national frameworks. Network States build legitimacy from the community upward and seek recognition externally.
- **vs. SEZs**: SEZs offer fiscal incentives within existing national boundaries and primarily serve businesses, not residential communities. Network States build full societies with culture, education, and social infrastructure.
- **vs. Digital Nomad Communities**: Digital nomads are individuals optimizing for personal freedom. Network States are collective entities building shared institutions and seeking political recognition.

## 3. WHY TRADITIONAL GDP DOES NOT APPLY

### 3.1 The Fundamental Mismatch

GDP (Gross Domestic Product) measures the total monetary value of all finished goods and services produced within a country's borders in a specific time period. This framework fails for Network States on at least seven dimensions:

**1. No contiguous territory.** GDP is measured within geographic borders. Network States are archipelagos -- distributed parcels of territory across multiple countries. A Network School member in Forest City contributes to Malaysia's GDP, but their economic output is generated for and through a community spanning 70+ countries.

**2. Members generate value across jurisdictions.** A Praxis member in Berlin founding a startup serves Praxis's collective economy but generates GDP for Germany. The economic value attributable to the Network State -- the network effects, the connections, the shared resources that enabled that startup -- is invisible to any national GDP calculation.

**3. Digital-first economic activity.** Much Network State economic activity occurs online: cryptocurrency transactions, remote work, digital products, on-chain governance. These flows may not register in any national accounts, or may register in the wrong jurisdiction.

**4. Non-market value creation.** Network States create significant value through education, mentorship, community building, and knowledge sharing that has no market price. A guest lecture from Vitalik Buterin at Network School has enormous economic value (comparable to a university education) but is not sold on an open market.

**5. Network effects are not captured.** The value of connecting a Korean founder with an Australian investor at Network School is not measured by any existing metric, yet may generate millions in future economic activity.

**6. Population is fluid.** GDP per capita requires a stable denominator. Network State members join, leave, participate part-time, or hold multiple community memberships simultaneously.

**7. Value accrues to a network, not a place.** When a startup incubated at Network School raises a Series A, that capital formation is credited to the startup's jurisdiction of incorporation, not to the network that enabled it.

### 3.2 The Measurement Gap

The UN Secretary-General's High-Level Expert Group on Beyond GDP released their first interim report at the 2025 World Summit for Social Development, acknowledging the need for indicators beyond GDP that capture well-being, equality, sustainability, governance, and resilience. The 2025 update to the UN System of National Accounts integrates digitalization and well-being considerations. A 2025 study in the American Economic Journal devised GDP-B to account for the welfare value of new and free goods.

Yet none of these frameworks address the specific challenge of measuring economic output for a **geographically distributed, voluntary, digitally-native community** -- which is precisely what Network States are.

Sources:
- https://unctad.org/news/beyond-gdp-what-else-matters-and-how-to-measure-it
- https://www.weforum.org/stories/2020/02/beyond-gdp-put-alternatives-economics-growth/

## 4. PROPOSED FRAMEWORK: THE NETWORK STATE ECONOMIC INDEX (NSEI)

We propose ten metrics organized into three tiers, progressing from directly measurable to derived indicators. Together, they form the **Network State Economic Index (NSEI)** -- a composite measure designed to be the GDP equivalent for Network States.

### Tier 1: Directly Measurable Metrics

#### 4.1 Gross Community Product (GCP)

**Definition:** The total economic output generated by all members of the Network State, regardless of physical location, that is attributable to or enabled by membership in the community.

**Components:**
- Revenue of businesses founded within the community
- Income earned by members through community-facilitated connections
- Membership fees and dues (direct community revenue)
- Investment capital deployed within the community
- Value of goods and services exchanged between members

**Calculation:**
> GCP = Sum(Member Business Revenue) + Sum(Member Income Attributable to Network) + Community Revenue + Internal Transaction Volume

**Why this matters:** GCP captures the economic footprint of the network regardless of geography. If a Network School alumnus in Seoul raises $5M for a startup conceived at Forest City, that $5M is part of the community's GCP.

**Measurement challenge:** Attribution. Not all member economic activity is caused by the network. A discount factor or survey-based attribution model would be needed.

**Proxy for early-stage Network States:** Monthly membership fees x 12 x multiplier. If Network School charges $1,500/month to 400 members, direct annual community revenue is $7.2 million. Applying a conservative 5x multiplier (based on the ratio of economic activity to membership fees in co-working accelerators) suggests a GCP proxy of ~$36 million.

#### 4.2 Capital Formation Rate

**Definition:** Total investment capital raised by startups and projects founded or incubated within the Network State per period.

**Examples:**
- Praxis: $544 million raised; member companies claim $1.117 trillion aggregate valuation
- Prospera: $100-200 million in total investment
- Network School: Individual startups raising from guest-lecturer-connected VCs (Sequoia, Polychain, Dragonfly)

**Benchmark comparison:**
- Y Combinator (5,000+ companies): $600 billion+ combined valuation, 82 unicorns
- Network School at scale could be compared to an accelerator cohort's output

#### 4.3 Cross-Border Transaction Volume

**Definition:** Total value of economic transactions between members of the Network State across national borders.

**Components:**
- Cryptocurrency transfers between members
- Service payments between members in different countries
- Investment flows from member investors to member startups
- Inter-node transfers (e.g., Network School Malaysia to future Network School Dubai)

**Why this matters:** Cross-border transaction volume is a direct measure of the network's function as an economic entity distinct from any single national economy. High internal transaction volume signals a functioning internal economy.

#### 4.4 Physical Footprint

**Definition:** Total land area, building floor space, and infrastructure value controlled by the Network State.

**Current examples:**
- Network School: Leased facilities in Forest City (building floor space TBD)
- CityDAO: 40 acres in Wyoming
- Prospera: Multiple buildings on Roatan
- Praxis/Atlas: Planned city near Vandenberg, California

**Growth rate** is as important as absolute size -- tracking quarter-over-quarter and year-over-year expansion of physical footprint.

### Tier 2: Network and Community Metrics

#### 4.5 Network Value (Modified Metcalfe's Law)

**Definition:** The theoretical value of the network based on the number of members and the quality of connections between them.

**Classical Metcalfe's Law:** V = n^2, where n = number of connected users. For Network School with 400 members: V proportional to 160,000 connection-pairs.

**Modified formula for Network States:**
> Network Value = n^2 * D * Q

Where:
- n = active members
- D = diversity coefficient (nationalities represented / total members; higher diversity = more valuable cross-border connections)
- Q = quality coefficient (average credentials, experience, or "talent density" per member)

**Critique:** Odlyzko et al. argue network value grows closer to n * log(n) rather than n^2, because not all connections are equally valuable. For Network States, this criticism is partially addressed by the diversity and quality multipliers -- connections between a Korean biotech founder and an Australian fintech investor are genuinely more valuable than random pairings.

**Benchmark:** Andreessen Horowitz identifies 16 metrics for measuring network effects, including organic vs. paid user share, retention cohort improvement, and direct traffic growth. These can be adapted for Network States by tracking member retention rates, referral rates, and engagement metrics.

Sources:
- https://a16z.com/16-ways-to-measure-network-effects/
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metcalfe%27s_law

#### 4.6 Talent Density

**Definition:** Skills, credentials, experience, and productive capacity per capita within the Network State.

**Measurable components:**
- Degrees and certifications per member
- Years of professional experience (aggregate and average)
- Number of founders / company builders per capita
- Number of published researchers per capita
- Programming languages / technical skills per capita
- Previous exits or successful ventures per capita

**Why this matters:** Traditional jurisdictions measure GDP per capita as a productivity proxy. For Network States, where the "product" is human capital mobilization and knowledge creation, talent density is a more direct measure of productive capacity.

**Network School example:** Members include founders, engineers, researchers, and investors from 70+ countries who have been selected through an application process. This self-selection creates talent density far exceeding the average for any country or city.

#### 4.7 Governance Participation Rate

**Definition:** Percentage of members actively participating in governance decisions (voting, proposals, contributions).

**Benchmark data (DAO ecosystem, 2025):**
- Average DAO voter participation: 17%
- Leading DAOs (Aave, MakerDAO): 22-28% on critical votes
- Most DAOs: Below 18%
- Top 20% of stakeholders hold 78% of tokens

**For Network States, governance participation would include:**
- Voting on community decisions
- Attending community meetings or town halls
- Submitting proposals
- Contributing to shared projects or infrastructure
- Serving in community roles

**Target benchmark:** A healthy Network State should aim for 30%+ governance participation, exceeding DAO averages, as physical colocation and shared mission should increase engagement relative to purely virtual DAOs.

Sources:
- https://patentpc.com/blog/dao-growth-stats-treasury-sizes-governance-votes-activity
- https://coinlaw.io/decentralized-autonomous-organizations-statistics/

#### 4.8 Immigration and Emigration Rates

**Definition:** Net growth in active membership per period.

**Immigration rate** = New members joining per quarter / Total members at start of quarter

**Emigration rate** = Members leaving per quarter / Total members at start of quarter

**Net migration** = Immigration rate - Emigration rate

**Why this matters:** Net migration is the most honest signal of community value. People vote with their feet (and their wallets). A Network State with high immigration and low emigration is creating value its members cannot find elsewhere. A Network State with high emigration has a problem no amount of treasury or territory can solve.

**Network School context:** The progression from 128 (Q4 2024) to 256 (March 2025) to 400+ (mid-2025) suggests strong immigration. Retention data between cohorts would be the critical complementary metric.

### Tier 3: Derived and Comparative Metrics

#### 4.9 Token/Treasury Value (Where Applicable)

**Definition:** For Network States with integrated cryptocurrencies, tokens, or DAOs, the total value of the community treasury and associated token market capitalization.

**DAO treasury benchmarks (2025):**
- Total DAO treasury assets globally: ~$24.5 billion
- Uniswap DAO: ~$2.9 billion
- MakerDAO: $8+ billion in collateral assets
- Top 5 DAOs control ~62.3% of all DAO treasury assets

**For Network States without tokens:** Treasury equivalent = total pooled financial resources + crowdfunded real estate value + shared infrastructure investment.

**CityDAO example:** $5 million treasury + 40 acres Wyoming land value.

#### 4.10 GCP Per Capita (Network State GDP Per Capita Equivalent)

**Definition:** Gross Community Product divided by active members.

> GCP Per Capita = GCP / Active Members

**This is the primary comparison metric** for benchmarking Network States against traditional jurisdictions.

**Illustrative calculation for Network School:**
- Direct revenue: 400 members x $1,500/month x 12 = $7.2M
- Estimated GCP (with 5x multiplier for economic activity): $36M
- GCP per capita: $36M / 400 = $90,000

**Comparison to traditional economies (2025 GDP per capita):**
- Singapore: $98,878
- UAE: ~$53,000
- Shenzhen: ~$35,000 (estimated)
- Malaysia: ~$13,000
- Honduras: ~$3,000

A GCP per capita of $90,000 for Network School would place it between Singapore and the UAE in per-capita economic output -- plausible given the self-selected, high-earning membership base, but this estimate requires validation through actual member economic survey data.

## 7. THE THESIS: WHY THIS MATTERS

### 7.1 Core Thesis

**Network States create economic value that is invisible to traditional measurement systems. They represent a new form of economic organization -- between companies and countries -- that requires new metrics to make their value visible and comparable.**

The economic output of a network is not the sum of transactions within its borders (it has no fixed borders). It is not the market capitalization of a token (many have no token). It is not the GDP of the territory it occupies (its members span dozens of jurisdictions).

The economic output of a Network State is the **total value created by the network's existence** -- the startups that would not have been founded, the investments that would not have been made, the research that would not have been produced, the connections that would not have been formed, absent the community.

### 7.2 Between Companies and Countries

| Attribute | Company | Network State | Country |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue model | Products/services | Membership + ecosystem | Taxation |
| Growth metric | Revenue/profit | GCP + network value | GDP |
| Population | Employees | Members | Citizens |
| Territory | Offices | Archipelago | Borders |
| Governance | Board + management | Social smart contract | Constitution |
| Exit option | Quit/sell shares | Leave community | Emigrate |
| Currency | Stock/equity | Token/crypto | National currency |

Network States occupy a genuinely novel position in economic taxonomy. They are too large and ambitious to be companies, too small and voluntary to be countries, too physical to be DAOs, and too distributed to be cities.

### 7.3 Policy Implications

**For DFAT and Australian foreign policy:**

1. **Trade agreements:** If Network States gain diplomatic recognition, bilateral trade agreements may need to accommodate non-territorial entities. Australia's 30+ members at Network School represent an Australian economic presence that is not captured by any bilateral trade statistics with Malaysia.

2. **Taxation:** Members of Network States pay taxes in their country of residence/citizenship, but the economic value they create is attributable to the network. This creates transfer pricing-style questions: if an Australian at Network School earns income through a connection made in the community, which jurisdiction captures the value?

3. **Regulation:** Network States operating within SEZs (like Forest City SFZ) create layered regulatory environments: national law (Malaysia) + SEZ framework (Forest City SFZ / JS-SEZ) + Network State norms (Network School). Regulators need to understand this layering.

4. **Soft power:** Australia's significant representation at Network School (30+ members, one of the largest national cohorts) represents a form of soft power projection that is currently untracked by DFAT. These Australians are building relationships with Korean, Indian, American, and global founders that may yield economic returns for decades.

5. **Economic measurement:** DFAT currently tracks bilateral trade in goods and services. It does not track the economic value of Australian participation in Network States. As these communities grow, this measurement gap will widen.

**For Malaysia and Forest City SFZ specifically:**

Network School's presence transforms Forest City from a "ghost city" with 2,000 maintenance workers into a hub of international economic activity. The 400+ high-income, high-skill members generate economic output (spending, business formation, tax revenue via the 15% knowledge worker rate) that materially contributes to the SFZ's viability.

**For global governance:**

The digital nomad economy already contributes an estimated $787-940 billion annually to the global economy, with 35+ million participants. Network States concentrate and organize this distributed economic activity into measurable communities. The 91% of nomad/remote-worker visas launched after 2020 signal government recognition of distributed economic actors. Network States are the next evolution of this trend.

## 9. SUMMARY: THE NSEI FRAMEWORK AT A GLANCE

### The Ten Metrics

| # | Metric | Tier | Type | Current Measurability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Gross Community Product (GCP) | 1 | Economic output | Medium (requires survey + records) |
| 2 | Capital Formation Rate | 1 | Investment | High (trackable via public records) |
| 3 | Cross-Border Transaction Volume | 1 | Internal economy | Low (requires on-chain + payment data) |
| 4 | Physical Footprint | 1 | Territory | High (real estate records) |
| 5 | Network Value | 2 | Network effects | Medium (Metcalfe formula + adjustments) |
| 6 | Talent Density | 2 | Human capital | Medium (member profiles + survey) |
| 7 | Governance Participation Rate | 2 | Engagement | High (voting records, attendance) |
| 8 | Immigration/Emigration Rates | 2 | Growth | High (membership records) |
| 9 | Token/Treasury Value | 3 | Financial reserves | High (on-chain for DAOs) |
| 10 | GCP Per Capita | 3 | Comparative | Medium (derived from GCP + members) |

### The Composite Index

> **NSEI = w1(GCP) + w2(Capital Formation) + w3(Transaction Volume) + w4(Footprint) + w5(Network Value) + w6(Talent Density) + w7(Governance) + w8(Net Migration) + w9(Treasury) + w10(GCP/capita)**

Weights (w1-w10) would be calibrated through empirical study across multiple Network States. Initial suggested weighting:
- Economic metrics (1-4): 50% weight
- Network metrics (5-8): 35% weight
- Derived metrics (9-10): 15% weight

### Application

The NSEI framework enables:
1. **Longitudinal tracking** -- Measuring a single Network State's economic development over time
2. **Cross-community comparison** -- Comparing Network School to Praxis to Prospera
3. **Cross-category comparison** -- Comparing a Network State to a city, SEZ, or DAO
4. **Policy input** -- Providing governments with data to inform taxation, regulation, and trade policy for these new entities
