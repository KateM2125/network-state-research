## 3. AUSTRALIAN AI COMPANIES WITH KOREAN CONNECTIONS

### Direct Korean Connections (Limited Evidence)
Research found limited publicly documented cases of Australian AI companies with direct Korean offices, Korean investors, or Korean founders. This represents an **opportunity gap** that the AI Bridge program could address.

### Australian Tech Companies with Potential Korean Market Presence
- **Canva** (Sydney): 230 million users in 190 countries, ARR exceeding $3 billion. Global presence likely includes Korean users, though no dedicated Korea office confirmed in search results.
- **Atlassian** (Sydney): Global SaaS company; Korean market presence via product distribution, not a dedicated Korea operation confirmed.
- **SafetyCulture** (Sydney/Townsville): 15,000+ organizations globally on platform; offices in Sydney, Townsville, Kansas City, Manchester, Manila. No Korea-specific office confirmed.

### K-Startup Grand Challenge (Korean Government Program)
- Korean government program actively recruiting Australian startups since 2016.
- In 2020, Collective Campus promoted the program to 3,000+ Australian/NZ startups, resulting in significantly more applications than ever before.
- 10+ Australian/NZ startups selected to pitch in 2020 round.
- Program provides: 3.5-month acceleration in Korea, initial funding, office space, 906 business meetings over three years with Korean companies, SMEs, and VCs.
  - Source: https://www.collectivecampus.io/blog/south-korea-once-again-on-the-lookout-for-australian-and-new-zealand-tech-startups
  - Source: https://www.k-startupgc.org/

### Austrade-Facilitated Technology Partnerships
- **Quantum Technology Business Roundtable (2025)**: Korea Quantum Industry Association (KQIA) and Austrade jointly hosted roundtable with Korean companies including LG Electronics and Noruma. Australian quantum companies visited Korea for direct engagement.
  - Source: https://thequantuminsider.com/2025/10/23/korea-australia-host-business-roundtable-to-strengthen-bilateral-quantum-technology-cooperation/

## 5. GOVERNMENT-LEVEL AI COOPERATION

### Foundational Frameworks

#### Australia-Korea Comprehensive Strategic Partnership (December 2021)
- Signed by PM Morrison and President Moon during state visit to Australia.
- **Three pillars**: (1) Strategic and security; (2) Economic, innovation and technology; (3) People-to-people exchange.
- Technology pillar includes: space development, cyber and critical technology, low/zero emissions technology, defence science and technology.
- **First Australia-ROK Tech-Bridge** launched September 2021 supporting collaborative research.
  - Source: https://www.dfat.gov.au/geo/republic-of-korea/republic-korea-south-korea/australia-republic-korea-comprehensive-strategic-partnership

#### MOU on Cyber and Critical Technology Cooperation (13 September 2021)
- Signed by FM Marise Payne and FM Chung Eui-yong in Seoul.
- Covers: next-generation telecommunications, **artificial intelligence**, quantum computing.
- Formalizes cooperation mechanisms and identifies key areas for elevated partnership.
  - Source: https://www.internationalcybertech.gov.au/Australia-and-Korea-sign-MoU

#### MOU on Digital Cooperation Initiative in Southeast Asia (13 December 2021)
- Second MOU signed during President Moon's state visit to Australia.
- Focuses on joint digital cooperation in Southeast Asian context.
  - Source: https://www.internationalcybertech.gov.au/node/155

### AI-Specific Government Cooperation

#### AI Seoul Summit (21-22 May 2024)
- Australia was one of 10 nations plus the EU to sign the **Seoul Declaration for Safe, Innovative and Inclusive AI**.
- Commitments include: supporting AI safety institutes, international cooperation on AI safety research, coordinating evidence-based approaches to AI safety.
- Australia signed both the Seoul Declaration (Leaders' Session, 21 May) and the Seoul Ministerial Statement (22 May, 27 countries + EU).
  - Source: https://www.industry.gov.au/news/australia-continues-commitment-safe-and-responsible-ai-through-seoul-declaration
  - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_Seoul_Summit

#### 6th Joint Committee on Science and Technology (3 December 2024, Sydney)
- First meeting since 2021. Australia and Korea exchanged updates on national S&T policy.
- **Explored opportunities for future collaboration, especially in quantum and AI.**
- Expressed interest in joint research, talent exchange, and innovation-driven partnerships.
  - Source: https://www.industry.gov.au/news/strengthening-australia-and-republic-koreas-research-partnership

#### Australia-ROK Critical Technologies Track 1.5 Dialogue (9 July 2024, Seoul)
- Co-hosted by ASPI and Korea's Science & Technology Policy Institute (STEPI).
- Brought together government, industry, and research stakeholders.
- Focus: biotechnology, AI, quantum, and space technologies for regional stability.
- Underpinned by the 2021 MOU on Cyber and Critical Technology Cooperation.
- Examined four lifecycle areas: biotech R&D, electric battery manufacturing, satellite launches, and **AI standards-setting**.
  - Source: https://www.aspi.org.au/news/aspi-co-hosts-australia-rok-critical-tech-track-1-5-in-seoul/

#### AI Standards Cooperation
- Recommended closer collaboration between **Standards Australia** and **Korea Standards Association** in establishing international AI standards.
- Coordination through ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 as a mechanism for harmonizing approaches.
  - Source: https://www.aspi.org.au/report/south-korea-and-australia-space-towards-strategic-partnership/

## 7. KEY FINDINGS AND IMPLICATIONS FOR THE AKF GRANT PROPOSAL

### Strengths to Emphasize
1. **The UNSW-KAIST MOU (2025)** is the flagship recent AU-KR university AI/tech partnership. The AI Bridge should position itself as complementary to and building on this momentum.

2. **Government frameworks are strong but implementation is thin.** The 2021 MOU on Cyber and Critical Technology, the Seoul Declaration, and the 6th JCST all identify AI as a priority, but operational programs delivering bilateral AI collaboration are scarce.

3. **The AKF Strategic Plan 2025-2030** explicitly names AI, robotics, cybersecurity, and advanced technologies as priority areas. The AI Bridge is directly aligned.

4. **Korea's National AI Research Lab** (KAIST-led, with POSTECH, Yonsei, Korea University) and the AER Labs affiliations with KAIST and SNU create a direct pipeline.

5. **The Anthropic connection is unique**: Daniel Kang as Anthropic Ambassador + ANU/Australian Government MOU with Anthropic under the National AI Plan creates an unmatched network position.

6. **Korean VC investment in Australian technology is a clear white space.** Hashed, SBVA, Kakao Ventures, and KIP are all active in Asia-Pacific but have no confirmed Australian portfolio companies. The investor showcase could catalyze first-mover connections.

7. **~30,000 Korean students study in Australia annually**, demonstrating strong people-to-people flow. The AI Bridge adds a professional/founder layer on top of the existing student pipeline.

### Gaps the AI Bridge Addresses
- No existing operational bilateral AI incubator between Australia and Korea
- Korean VC firms are active in Southeast Asia but not investing in Australian AI
- Australian AI companies are not systematically entering the Korean market
- University partnerships are strong on paper (exchanges, MOUs) but lack structured AI-specific programming
- Government frameworks (CSP, MOU on Cyber and Critical Tech) need operational delivery mechanisms

### Data Points for the Proposal
- Korea's AI market: ~US$5.5 billion (2024), projected to exceed US$53 billion by 2032
- Korea has 380+ AI companies, 149 funded startups with US$2.15 billion raised
- Korean FDI in Australia: US$900 million FY24
- KAFTA in force since 2014
- 5 Korean firms selected for sovereign AI initiative (Naver, LG, SK Telecom, Upstage, NC AI)
- Samsung AI: 50,000+ GPUs in AI factory
- KAIST National AI Research Lab: 45 professors, 150+ researchers, 12 domestic companies, 14 overseas partners
