## 1. Who Issues Recognition?

Recognition is not a single act. It is a layered bundle of decisions issued by different authorities, each carrying different legal weight. A Network State pursuing recognition must understand the layers because several can accumulate before the hardest (UN membership) becomes feasible.

| Issuer | Mechanism | Legal Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Individual state (bilateral) | Diplomatic note, establishment of embassy, exchange of ambassadors | Constitutive between the two parties; opposable to third parties only partially |
| UN General Assembly | Admission vote under Art. 4 UN Charter, 2/3 majority after UNSC recommendation | Near-universal de jure recognition; strongest signal available |
| UN specialised agencies (WHO, UNESCO, ICAO, UPU, ITU) | Separate admission votes per agency | Functional recognition; Palestine used UNESCO 2011 as precedent |
| Regional bodies (EU, AU, OAS, ASEAN, Council of Europe) | Membership or observer status | Strong regional legitimacy; EU requires unanimity among 27 |
| International Court of Justice | Contentious jurisdiction acceptance; advisory opinions | Quasi-judicial; Kosovo advisory opinion 2010 clarified but did not grant recognition |
| Functional recognition | Passport acceptance, currency convertibility, postal union, SWIFT/BIC code, ccTLD (ISO 3166-1) | Operational; accumulates without explicit political act |

Sources: UN Charter Art. 4 (https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-charter); ICJ Kosovo advisory opinion (https://www.icj-cij.org/case/141); ISO 3166 Maintenance Agency (https://www.iso.org/iso-3166-country-codes.html).

## 2. Declarative vs Constitutive Theory

Two doctrines dominate the literature. The **declarative theory**, codified in the Montevideo Convention 1933, holds that statehood exists objectively once the four criteria are met; recognition merely acknowledges the fact. The **constitutive theory** holds that a state exists only once recognised by other states — recognition creates the legal person. Contemporary practice is hybrid: Crawford (2006, *The Creation of States in International Law*, 2nd ed., Oxford) argues Montevideo is necessary but not sufficient, and that widespread non-recognition can defeat otherwise valid claims (see Taiwan, Somaliland).

Source: Montevideo Convention on the Rights and Duties of States, 26 Dec 1933 (https://www.oas.org/en/sla/dil/docs/inter_american_treaties_A-40_rights_duties_states.pdf); Crawford, J. (2006).

## 3. Montevideo Criteria and Digital Equivalents

| Criterion (Montevideo Art. 1) | Traditional Metric | Network-State Equivalent (bridge question) |
|---|---|---|
| Permanent population | Resident census, birth registry | Verified citizen roll with biometric or cryptographic identity; continuity of membership over time |
| Defined territory | Contiguous land, demarcated borders | Distributed land holdings, diplomatic enclaves, or treaty-secured jurisdiction; territorial nexus still expected by UN practice |
| Government | Functioning executive, legislature, courts | On-chain governance with enforceable decisions; recognised dispute-resolution authority |
| Capacity for foreign relations | Diplomatic corps, treaty-making power | Demonstrated treaty partners, embassy network, or agency membership |

Modern ICJ and UN practice has added implicit tests: effective control, permanence, and non-violation of *jus cogens* (e.g. self-determination, prohibition on aggression) — see ICJ *Kosovo* (2010) and *Chagos* (2019).

## 4. UN Membership Pathway

UN Charter Article 4 requires that applicants be (i) a state, (ii) peace-loving, (iii) accept Charter obligations, (iv) able and willing to carry them out. Procedure: UNSC recommendation (9 of 15 votes, no P5 veto), then UNGA admission by 2/3 majority of members present and voting.

| Case | Year | Outcome | Blocker |
|---|---|---|---|
| South Sudan | 2011 | Admitted (193rd member) | None after Sudanese consent |
| Palestine | 2012 | Non-member observer state (UNGA Res 67/19) | US veto threat at UNSC |
| Kosovo | ongoing | Not admitted | Russia/China veto; ~100 bilateral recognitions |
| Taiwan | expelled 1971 | No pathway | PRC occupies China seat (UNGA Res 2758) |

Sources: UN Charter (https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-charter); UNGA Res 67/19 (https://undocs.org/A/RES/67/19); UNGA Res 2758 (https://undocs.org/A/RES/2758(XXVI)).

## 5. Economic Indicators (Micro-State Floor)

Recognising states do not publish formal economic thresholds, but micro-state practice reveals a de facto floor.

| Indicator | Typical Floor | Micro-State Example |
|---|---|---|
| GDP | No minimum; Tuvalu ~USD 60m | Tuvalu (pop. ~11,000) |
| Currency | Own or formal adoption of foreign | San Marino uses EUR by agreement with EU; Kiribati uses AUD |
| Central bank / monetary authority | Required function, not institution | Nauru relies on Bendigo Bank; Tuvalu has no central bank |
| Sovereign debt issuance | Not required for recognition | Many micro-states have never issued bonds |
| Trade agreements | Typically 1+ | Nauru WTO observer only |
| Tax authority | Required | All recognised micro-states operate one |
| IMF/World Bank membership | Strong signal, not mandatory | Tuvalu joined IMF 2010, 35 years after independence |

Sources: IMF country data (https://www.imf.org/en/Countries); World Bank WDI (https://data.worldbank.org); San Marino–EU Monetary Agreement 2012 (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:22012A0317(01)).

## 6. Social Infrastructure Expectations

| Infrastructure | Minimum Viable Form | Existing-State Benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| Civil registry | Births/deaths/marriages authoritative record | UNSD Principles and Recommendations for Vital Statistics, Rev 3 |
| Healthcare | Primary-care access, vaccination programme | WHO Member State reporting |
| Education | Compulsory primary education | UNESCO ISCED levels 1-2 |
| Judiciary | Independent courts, appellate layer | Venice Commission Rule of Law Checklist 2016 |
| Dispute resolution | Binding, enforceable | New York Convention 1958 accession |
| Governance legitimacy | Periodic free elections or equivalent consent mechanism | ICCPR Art. 25 |
| Freedom of movement | Issuance of travel documents accepted abroad | ICAO Doc 9303 machine-readable passports |
| Citizenship regime | Defined acquisition/loss rules, non-statelessness | 1961 Convention on the Reduction of Statelessness |

Sources: UNSD (https://unstats.un.org/unsd/demographic-social/Standards-and-Methods/); ICAO Doc 9303 (https://www.icao.int/publications/pages/publication.aspx?docnum=9303); Venice Commission (https://www.venice.coe.int/images/SITE%20IMAGES/Publications/Rule_of_Law_Check_List.pdf).

## 7. Failure Modes and Partial Recognition

| Entity | Status | Primary Blocker |
|---|---|---|
| Taiwan (ROC) | De facto state, 12 de jure recognitions | PRC diplomatic pressure; UNGA Res 2758 |
| Kosovo | ~100 bilateral recognitions, no UN seat | Russia/China UNSC veto; Serbia non-recognition |
| Palestine | UNGA observer, 140+ bilateral | US UNSC veto; Israeli occupation |
| Western Sahara (SADR) | AU member, 40+ recognitions | Moroccan control; UN mission (MINURSO) stalled |
| Somaliland | Zero formal recognitions | AU preserves colonial borders; Somali objection |
| Northern Cyprus (TRNC) | Recognised only by Turkey | UNSC Res 541 (1983) declared invalid |

Sources: UNSC Res 541 (https://undocs.org/S/RES/541(1983)); Caspersen, N. (2012) *Unrecognized States*, Polity; AU Constitutive Act Art. 4(b).

## 8. Implications for Network States

Mapped against the matrix above, current Network State projects (Praxis, Afropolitan, Prospera charter-city, Zuzalu pop-ups) clear 2 of Montevideo's 4 criteria credibly — permanent population (via verified rolls) and arguably government (via on-chain governance). They fail, or are untested on, defined territory and capacity for foreign relations. On the economic floor, none has issued sovereign debt or secured a monetary agreement comparable to San Marino's. On the social floor, civil registry and citizenship regimes are the nearest-term wins; judicial enforceability and ICAO-compliant travel documents are the hardest. The binding question for Kate's next paper is therefore not whether Network States can simulate statehood, but whether any existing state will enter a **Monaco- or San Marino-style compact** that confers territorial nexus and treaty capacity without requiring contiguous land — the single manoeuvre that unlocks the remaining criteria.
